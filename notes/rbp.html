Security & Role-Based Permissions

Role-based permissions (RBP) answer three questions: who gets access (permission group), what they may do (permission role) and on whom (target population).

Quiz this topic

Key concepts

  • Permission group. A set of users defined by criteria such as department or location. Groups built on criteria are dynamic: when an employee's data changes, membership updates automatically. Maintained in Manage Permission Groups.
  • Permission role. A collection of permissions, e.g. view and edit of Job Information fields. Maintained in Manage Permission Roles.
  • Granting a role. A role is granted to a granted population (a permission group, everyone, or a relationship such as managers or HR managers) together with a target population: the users the granted users can act on.
  • Relationship-based access. Roles can be granted to managers, HR managers, matrix managers and custom managers (e.g. a second manager), with target population defined as their reports, optionally several levels deep.
  • Exclude self. The option to exclude granted users from having the permission access to themselves stops, for example, a manager from editing their own salary through the manager role.
  • Permission categories. Field-level permissions for effective-dated entities such as Job Information or Compensation Information sit under Employee Central Effective Dated Entities. Non-effective-dated entities sit under Employee Data.
  • Securing MDF objects. A custom MDF object only appears in permission roles after you set Secured = Yes and pick a permission category in Configure Object Definitions.
  • Troubleshooting. View User Permission shows which permissions a user has and through which roles they were granted.

Common traps

  • Granted population receives the permission; target population is who they act on. Exam scenarios like to swap the two.
  • A field you set to edit in the data model is still invisible if no role grants view or edit on it.
  • A new custom MDF object is not permissioned until it is secured.

Practice tasks

Do these in a practice or demo instance. Each one ends with how to prove the outcome, just like in the exam.

HR for one country

Outcome: Members of a new group HR Germany can view and edit Job Information of all employees in German legal entities, and nobody else gets this access.

Hints
  1. Create a dynamic permission group by criteria (e.g. legal entity or country).
  2. Create a role with Job Information view and edit under Employee Central Effective Dated Entities.
  3. Grant it to the group with a target population of German employees.

Prove it: In View User Permission, pick a group member and show the Job Information permission and the role it comes from. Check that a non-member does not have it.

Managers without self-service salary edits

Outcome: Managers can edit compensation of their direct reports but not their own.

Hints
  1. Grant the role to managers with target population of direct reports.
  2. Use the option that excludes granted users from accessing themselves.

Prove it: Proxy as a manager (if allowed) or use View User Permission to compare the manager's access on a report and on themselves.

Permission a custom object

Outcome: A custom MDF object can be viewed by HR only.

Hints
  1. Secure the object and give it a permission category in Configure Object Definitions.
  2. Add view/edit for the object to the HR role.

Prove it: Show the object in the permission role and open a record in Manage Data as an HR user.

Employee Data & Imports

How employee records are structured, how they change over time, and how to load them in bulk.

Quiz this topic

Key concepts

  • Person vs. employment. Personal information, addresses and national ID belong to the person. Job, compensation and employment details belong to an employment, so one person can have several employments.
  • Effective dating. Job Information, Compensation Information, Personal Information and Addresses are effective dated and keep history. Email, phone and National ID are not.
  • Hiring. Add New Employee runs a duplicate check (name, date of birth, national ID) to avoid duplicate persons and support rehires.
  • Changing data. Managers start job changes such as promotions through Take Action → Change Job and Compensation Info on the employee's profile.
  • Import order. The Basic Import (user data) comes first; other employee imports such as Biographical Information, Employment Details and Job History depend on it.
  • Full Purge vs. Incremental. Full Purge replaces the existing records of that entity for the employees in the file. Incremental Load adds or updates records.
  • Keeping values. In an incremental import, &&NO_OVERWRITE&& keeps a field's current value instead of overwriting it.

Common traps

  • Full Purge only affects the employees in the file — but for them it replaces all records of that entity.
  • An empty cell in an incremental import is not the same as &&NO_OVERWRITE&&.
  • Use Import Employee Data for employee entities, Import and Export Data for MDF objects.

Practice tasks

Do these in a practice or demo instance. Each one ends with how to prove the outcome, just like in the exam.

Hire end to end

Outcome: A new employee exists with personal data, job data and compensation, effective on the first of next month.

Hints
  1. Use Add New Employee.
  2. Watch for the duplicate check.
  3. Complete job and compensation blocks.

Prove it: Open the profile and show Job History with the Hire event on the right date.

Bulk update without data loss

Outcome: Twenty employees get a new cost center from a file, while their other job fields stay unchanged.

Hints
  1. Download the Job History template from Import Employee Data.
  2. Use Incremental Load and &&NO_OVERWRITE&& for fields you don't change.

Prove it: Open Job History of two employees from the file and show the new record with only the cost center changed.

A promotion with history

Outcome: An employee is promoted with a new job, pay grade and salary from a future date.

Hints
  1. Take Action → Change Job and Compensation Info.
  2. Set the effective date and both blocks in one transaction.

Prove it: Show the new and the previous records in Job History and Compensation history.
